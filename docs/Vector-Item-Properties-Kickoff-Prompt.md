## Decisions to surface

Apply the two-part test from the global guidance. Four decisions are expected to pass it; present all four with the consequential decision template before Phase 1 step 2 and wait.

- **1. Where the shared properties live for the text items.** Technical Architecture PRD 4.2 lists `TextItem` and `CalloutItem` under `VectorItem`; the code has them under `SnapGraphicsItem` with `RichTextMixin`, with their own `bg_color`, `border_color`, `border_width`, and `border_style`, and the Property Panel's Appearance section does not show for them (their colours live in the Text section, General UI PRD 8.4). Option A, move them under `VectorItem`: `bg_color` becomes `fill_color`, `border_*` become `stroke_*`, Appearance shows for a text box and a callout, and 4.2 is met; its cost is every text and callout test, the Text section's Background and Border rows (which would now duplicate Appearance's), the Snagit reader and writer's field names, and old files' keys read as fallbacks. Option B, keep them where they are and mix in `ShadowMixin` and the two opacities directly, as the stamp and emoji items do, with the Text section keeping Background and Border: the shadow and the opacities arrive, Appearance stays hidden for text items, and 4.2 gains a row saying the two text items sit beside `VectorItem` rather than under it; its cost is that "vector item" means two things (the class and the property set) and that the panel's Fill Opacity and Stroke Opacity for a text box live in the Text section. Option C, defer the text items to the following kickoff: smallest; its cost is that the Text PRD's 3.6 ("Transform, Appearance, Shadow, and Text sections") and 8.3 ("Shadow renders correctly for the combined bubble + tail shape") stay unmet. Recommendation: B; the property set is what the PRDs and the panel need, and the class move would churn the largest test surfaces in the repository for a naming change.
- **2. What the vector items' Opacity control becomes.** General UI PRD 5.2 and 8.3 say Fill Opacity and Stroke Opacity are shown for vector items and non-vector items show a single Opacity "instead"; Technical Architecture PRD 3.1.4 says vector items use the two "instead" of the base opacity. Today every vector tool has `opacity_pct` in its creation defaults, the bar shows one Opacity slider for it, the Appearance section shows one Opacity slider, and presets and themes on disk carry `opacity_pct`. Option A, replace: vector items and their tools expose `fill_opacity` and `stroke_opacity` and no `opacity_pct`; Appearance shows the two sliders for a vector selection and the one slider for a non-vector selection; a stored preset's or theme's `opacity_pct` for a shape tool is read once into both new keys and dropped on the next write; the base `opacity` stays as a Qt property for groups, non-vector items, and the layer machinery. Its cost is the migration and the changed meaning of a saved preset. Option B, keep all three: the two new sliders join Opacity; its cost is three opacity controls on a rectangle, the deviation the numbered step already carries, and a PRD 8.3 wording departed from for good. Recommendation: A; the PRDs are unanimous and the migration is one codec rule.
- **3. How far into the Basic Shape tools this kickoff goes.** The shared properties are one commit per item; the Basic Shape PRD also specifies arrowheads (4.3 to 4.7), corner radius modes (5.3, 5.4), point editing (3.5, 8.5, 9.7), curved and elbow arrows (4.5, 4.6), the Arc and Polygon tools (7, 8), and the Freehand tool's Bezier pipeline (9.3). Option A, the shared properties only (Phases 1 and 2): every shape gains style, cap, join, opacities, and shadow, and the per-tool controls General UI 5.3 omitted stay omitted; its cost is that the Arrow tool still draws one fixed head and the Rectangle's corner radius stays unreachable from the bar. Option B, Phases 1 to 3 as written above (arrowheads with straight lines only, the uniform corner radius control): its cost is a third phase of geometry work in a properties kickoff, and two PRD sections (4.5, 4.6, 5.3's individual mode) recorded as partly built. Option C, the whole Basic Shape PRD in one kickoff: its cost is two new tools with their own state machines and point editing on every shape, several weeks of scope in one notes document, and the palette count changing again (twenty-one tools). Recommendation: B; the arrowheads and the corner radius are the two controls users meet first, and the rest is a kickoff of its own with its own decisions (tool count, point-editing handles shared with the callout's tail).
- **4. Whether the item blend mode is built for every item or the Highlighter alone.** Technical Architecture PRD 3.1.4 puts `blend_mode` on every item and General UI PRD 8.3 puts a Blend Mode dropdown in Appearance; the Blur PRD's Highlighter has its own `blend_mode` with multiply by default; the layer's blend mode is already applied per item as the painter composition mode (follow-up decision 3). Option A, `blend_mode` on `SnapGraphicsItem` for every item: the Appearance dropdown lists Normal plus the layer's seven names, `_apply_flip` sets the item's mode when the layer's is Normal and the layer's otherwise (one composition mode per paint), and the Highlighter's default is multiply; its cost is the combination rule, a serialized key on every item, and the SVG export dropping it for every item. Option B, the Highlighter alone (Blur PRD 3.4's four modes on `HighlightItem`, its bar and panel row): its cost is that 3.1.4 and 8.3 stay departed from for the other items, with rows. Recommendation: A; the machinery exists on the base since the follow-up, and the rule is one sentence.

Everything else follows the PRDs; where they are silent or disagree, decide, note it under the notes' decisions section and the PRD rows, and continue. Twelve silences are known:

- The Snagit writer: `stroke_style`, the opacities, and the shadow are not mapped (the writer maps what it maps today); a Snagit notes row.
- The item blend mode's combination rule of decision 4: the item's mode when the layer's is Normal, the layer's otherwise; recorded in Technical Architecture PRD 3.9.
- Groups: a group has no properties of its own; Appearance and Shadow for a selected group act on its vector members as today (`_selected_vectors`) and on its shadow-carrying members (`_selected_shadowed` expanding groups), with the 8.6 mixed indicators.
